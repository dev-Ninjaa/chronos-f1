# FIA Formula 1 Technical Regulations - Key Excerpts

## DRS (Drag Reduction System) Rules

### Article 11.2 - DRS Activation
The DRS (Drag Reduction System) may only be activated by the driver in the pre-determined DRS zones when:
- The driver is within one second of the car ahead at the DRS detection point
- The race has been running for at least two laps after the start or restart
- No yellow flags are being displayed in the DRS zone
- Track conditions are declared dry by Race Control

### Article 11.3 - DRS Restrictions
DRS must be disabled:
- During Safety Car periods
- During Virtual Safety Car periods
- During yellow flag conditions
- During the first two laps after race start
- During the first two laps after a restart

## Tyre Regulations

### Article 24.1 - Tyre Compounds
Each driver must use at least two different specifications of dry-weather tyres during the race, unless:
- The race is suspended and cannot be restarted
- The race is declared wet by Race Control
- The driver fails to complete more than 50% of race distance

### Article 24.2 - Tyre Allocation
For each race weekend, drivers receive:
- 13 sets of dry-weather tyres (combination of Soft, Medium, Hard)
- 4 sets of intermediate tyres
- 3 sets of wet-weather tyres

### Article 24.3 - Tyre Pressure
Minimum starting tyre pressures are defined by the FIA for each race:
- Front tyres: Typically 19.0-21.0 PSI
- Rear tyres: Typically 18.0-20.0 PSI
These values vary by circuit and are communicated in the race director's notes.

## Pit Stop Regulations

### Article 23.1 - Pit Lane Speed Limit
The pit lane speed limit is 80 km/h (49.7 mph) unless otherwise specified by the FIA for a particular circuit. Exceeding this limit results in:
- First offense: Time penalty (typically 5 seconds)
- Subsequent offenses: Drive-through penalty or stop-and-go penalty

### Article 23.2 - Pit Stop Procedure
During a pit stop:
- The car must be stationary before any work begins
- A maximum of 5 mechanics may work on each wheel simultaneously
- The driver must not release the car until all wheel guns are clear
- The car must not be released into the path of another car

### Article 23.3 - Unsafe Release
Releasing a car in an unsafe manner results in:
- Minimum 10-second stop-and-go penalty
- Possible further penalties if deemed dangerous
- Team may be fined up to €25,000

## Safety Car Regulations

### Article 39.1 - Safety Car Deployment
The Safety Car may be deployed when:
- Track conditions are unsafe for racing
- An incident requires marshals on track
- Debris needs to be cleared
- Weather conditions deteriorate suddenly

### Article 39.2 - Safety Car Procedure
When the Safety Car is deployed:
- All cars must reduce speed and form up behind the Safety Car
- Overtaking is forbidden except to regain lost positions
- Lapped cars may be instructed to unlap themselves
- Pit lane remains open unless specifically closed

### Article 39.3 - Safety Car Restart
The Safety Car will return to the pits when:
- Track conditions are safe
- All marshals are clear
- Race Control determines racing can resume
The Safety Car lights will be extinguished on the lap before it returns to the pits.

## Virtual Safety Car (VSC)

### Article 39.12 - VSC Activation
The Virtual Safety Car may be used for:
- Incidents requiring brief track intervention
- Situations not severe enough for full Safety Car
- Quick debris removal

### Article 39.13 - VSC Procedure
During VSC:
- Drivers must maintain a target lap time set by Race Control
- Delta times are displayed on steering wheels
- Overtaking is forbidden
- Pit lane remains open
- Cars must maintain relative positions

## Flag Signals

### Article 2.5 - Flag Meanings

**Green Flag:** Track is clear, racing conditions normal

**Yellow Flag:** Danger on or near the track
- Single waved: Reduce speed, be prepared to change direction, no overtaking
- Double waved: Reduce speed significantly, be prepared to stop, no overtaking

**Red Flag:** Session suspended
- All cars must return to pit lane immediately
- No overtaking permitted

**Blue Flag:** Faster car approaching to lap
- Must be shown for at least 3 marshalling posts
- Driver must allow faster car to pass within 3 blue flag displays
- Failure to comply: Penalty (typically 5 seconds)

**Black and White Flag (Diagonal):** Warning for unsportsmanlike behavior
- Shown with car number
- Next offense may result in penalty

**Black Flag with Orange Circle:** Mechanical problem detected
- Shown with car number
- Driver must return to pits immediately

## Penalty System

### Article 38.3 - Time Penalties
**5-Second Penalty:** Added to race time or served at pit stop
- Minor track limits violations
- Minor contact with another car
- First pit lane speeding offense

**10-Second Penalty:** Added to race time or served at pit stop
- More serious track limits violations
- Causing a collision
- Gaining lasting advantage by leaving track

**Drive-Through Penalty:** Must drive through pit lane at speed limit
- Serious pit lane speeding
- Ignoring blue flags
- Unsafe release from pit stop

**Stop-and-Go Penalty:** Must stop in pit box for specified time (10-30 seconds)
- Very serious offenses
- Dangerous driving
- Multiple offenses

### Article 38.4 - Grid Penalties
Applied to starting position for next race:
- Engine component changes beyond allocation
- Gearbox changes before required mileage
- Accumulated penalty points

## Weight Regulations

### Article 4.1 - Minimum Weight
The minimum weight of the car, without fuel but with driver, must not be less than:
- 798 kg (2024 regulations)
This includes:
- Driver in racing suit
- Seat
- All fluids except fuel

### Article 4.2 - Ballast
If the car is underweight:
- Ballast may be added
- Must be secured in a way that requires tools to remove
- Must be placed as low as possible in the car

## Fuel Regulations

### Article 6.5 - Fuel Capacity
Maximum fuel capacity: 110 kg at race start

### Article 6.6 - Fuel Flow Rate
Maximum fuel flow rate: 100 kg/hour

### Article 6.7 - Fuel Samples
The FIA may take fuel samples at any time to verify compliance with regulations.

## Power Unit Regulations

### Article 5.1 - Power Unit Components
Each driver is allocated per season:
- 3 Internal Combustion Engines (ICE)
- 3 Motor Generator Unit-Kinetic (MGU-K)
- 3 Motor Generator Unit-Heat (MGU-H)
- 3 Turbochargers (TC)
- 2 Energy Stores (ES)
- 2 Control Electronics (CE)
- 8 Exhaust Systems

Exceeding allocation results in grid penalties.

## Aerodynamic Testing Restrictions

### Article 22.1 - Wind Tunnel Testing
Teams are allocated aerodynamic testing time based on championship position:
- 1st place: 70% of baseline
- 2nd place: 75% of baseline
- 3rd place: 80% of baseline
- And so on...

This promotes competitive balance.

## Parc Fermé Regulations

### Article 34.2 - Parc Fermé Conditions
From the start of qualifying until the start of the race:
- Cars are in parc fermé
- Only specific changes are permitted
- Setup changes are forbidden
- Tyre pressures may be adjusted
- Fuel may be added or removed

## Race Start Procedure

### Article 36.9 - Starting Grid
Cars must be in their grid positions:
- 15 minutes before start time
- Engines may be started
- Mechanics must leave grid 3 minutes before start
- Any car not in position: Start from pit lane

### Article 36.13 - Start Lights
The start sequence:
- 5 red lights illuminate one by one
- Random delay (typically 1-5 seconds)
- All lights extinguish simultaneously
- Race begins when lights go out

## Track Limits

### Article 27.3 - Leaving the Track
A driver will be judged to have left the track if:
- No part of the car remains in contact with the track
- All four wheels are beyond the white line

### Article 27.4 - Gaining an Advantage
If a driver leaves the track and gains a lasting advantage:
- Must give back the position
- Or receive a time penalty
- Three violations: Black and white flag warning
- Continued violations: Time penalty
